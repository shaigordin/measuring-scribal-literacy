Count only what is on the clay

A sign counts if it is on the tablet, even damaged.

A word counts if half its signs survive, or if it is readable elsewhere on the same tablet.

Restorations from a duplicate never count. They show the comparative text, not what is on the tablet.

A word without analysis takes that of the same spelling elsewhere, or its eDiAna lemma (Luwian).

[grey] = restored by the editor, ⸢ ⸣ = damaged

Orphans and Archetypes · Mainz, 18 November 2026

TLHdig marks restorations with square brackets and damaged signs with half brackets. Restorations made from duplicates or parallels are not used, so the method cannot simply find the text the editor restored from. Rule: qtm/variants.py (the dataset without restorations). Over the corpus: 958,323 of 1,002,347 analysed words count; 47,090 of them only through the same text. Damaged signs and uncertain readings (?) count; omitted signs (〈 〉) do not. Fills (fill_lemmas.py, ediana_cluwian.py): of 1,277,944 legible words, 135,398 had no analysis in TLHdig 0.3; 94,021 take the analysis of the same spelling elsewhere in TLHdig, 1,109 Cuneiform Luwian words take their lemma from eDiAna; 40,268 remain without one.
