\title{Orphans and Archetypes: Placing Unassigned Hittite Ritual Fragments\\ in Their Traditions}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Thousands of Hittite ritual and festival fragments cannot be assigned to a composition because no
parallel passage survives. Laroche's residual category \CTH{470} and the recently digitised EBo
fragments hold \Q{orph.orphans_total} such manuscripts in the TLHdig 0.3 release, with a median of
\Q{orph.cth470_median} legible words in \CTH{470} and \Q{orph.ebo_median} among the EBo. We ask whether the words of a
fragment and the way they are spelled can place it in a ritual or festival tradition, and how far
such a placement can be trusted. Ten reference traditions are taken from the studies that argued
for them and mapped to Laroche's CTH numbers. Nearest-centroid assignment over tf-idf features of
word forms and sign sequences returns \Q{E1.both.acc} of held-out manuscripts to their tradition
(majority baseline \Q{E1.both.majority}), and \Q{E10a.compositions_correct} of
\Q{E10a.compositions} compositions held out whole return to their scholarly group. A margin
calibrated on held-out fragments of matching size lets the method abstain; it places
\Q{E5.assigned} of \Q{corpus.orphans_used} orphans at an expected precision of
\Q{E2.precision}. Splitting each decision exactly over its features shows that traditions are
identified by what is written, above all agents, deities, realia and foreign-language words, and
hardly by how it is spelled; common particles make up \Q{E11b.common.similarity} of the
similarity but \Q{E11b.common.ref} of the decision. Code and outputs are released, and every
number in the paper is generated from them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Emmanuel Laroche's \textit{Catalogue des textes hittites} \citep{laroche1971catalogue} gave every
Hittite composition a number, and with it a place: rituals in one block, festivals in another,
texts in Hattian, Luwian and Hurrian in blocks of their own. What could not be placed went into
residual categories. \CTH{470}, ``fragments of rituals'', is the largest of them, and it has grown
with every volume of hand copies since 1971. The Mainz Hethitologie-Portal keeps Laroche's numbers
as a shared address system while moving fragments after joins \citep{cthportal,konkordanz}. The
digitisation of the EBo fragments in TLHdig 0.3 \citep{tlhdig03} adds \Q{orph.ebo_texts} more
manuscripts with a median of \Q{orph.ebo_median} legible words, most of them filed under two
further residual numbers, \CTH{832} and \CTH{670}.

A philologist assigns such a fragment through a parallel: a duplicate, or a close parallel
passage in an identified composition. For most of these orphans that route is closed by
definition. We ask instead whether the content of a fragment, its words and their spellings, can
point to its tradition, and how far such a pointer can be trusted. Put this way, the task is
attribution, and two known weaknesses of attribution apply with full force: accuracy falls with
the length of the sample \citep{eder2015size}, and a classifier with a closed set of candidates
always names one of them, even when the true source is not among them \citep{koppel2011wild}.
A placement is also only useful to a philologist who can check it, which requires knowing what
it rests on.

\citet{gordin2025matters} mapped about 1,000 Hurrian and Luwian festival manuscripts with tf-idf
and t-SNE and proposed, as a next step, to test which word groups carry the signal. We turn that
exploration into assignment, with explicit baselines, held-out evaluation and a rule for when not
to assign. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a reference of \Q{trad.countword} ritual and festival traditions, each defined by the
    study that argued for it and mapped to Laroche's CTH numbers, with the ranges that no study
    claims set aside as test sets (Section~\ref{sec:data});
  \item an evaluation that holds out manuscripts and whole compositions, so that the scholarly
    groupings themselves, and one contested attribution, are tested; that follows accuracy
    down to fragment size; and that calibrates a margin for abstention
    (Section~\ref{sec:results});
  \item an exact decomposition of each decision over word categories and writing layers, which
    separates what is written from how it is written and shows how much rests on common
    particles such as \textit{nu} (Section~\ref{sec:decision});
  \item a placement of \Q{E5.assigned} orphans, each with the features it rests on, as leads
    for verification on the tablet.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Traditions and the catalogue.}
Laroche sorted by genre first and by language second, while later scholarship defines a ritual
tradition by the origin of its practitioners, its cult and its ritual language. One tradition
therefore ends up scattered over several CTH ranges. The Kizzuwatna rituals occupy
\CTH{471}--500, but the ritual of the Kizzuwatnean Old Woman Maštigga is filed with the named
practitioners at \CTH{404} \citep{miller2004studies}; the rituals of the AZU priest (\CTH{701})
sit in the Hurrian block \citep{salvini1986chs}; and the named-practitioner rituals of
\CTH{390}--469 come from Arzawa, the Lower Land and Kizzuwatna alike
\citep{christiansen2006ambazzi,bawanypeck2005auguren}. The festivals have been regrouped by cult
geography \citep{gordin2025matters,gorke2022hurrian,trameri2024kizzuwatna} and, in the
Hethitische Festrituale project (HFR), into edition modules such as the Luwian and the Palaic
festivals \citep{hfr,gorke2020hatti}.

\paragraph{Scribal habit and attribution.}
Hittite scribes copied, excerpted and re-edited ritual texts over two centuries, and their
habits of logographic writing, plene spelling and sign choice vary with date, school and
individual \citep{gordin2015scribal,weeden2011logograms,vandenhout2009dating,waal2015diplomatics}.
Whether a habit marks a tradition, a circle of scribes or a date is therefore a question to test,
not an assumption. Stylometry supplies frequency-based representations, distance measures and
protocols with held-out evaluation \citep{burrows2002delta,juola2008authorship,
stamatatos2009survey}, and open-set recognition the means to reject a sample that fits no
candidate \citep{scheirer2013openset}.

\paragraph{Computational work on cuneiform.}
Machine learning on cuneiform has addressed transliteration and translation
\citep{gordin2020reading,yavasan2025clay}, language and dialect identification
\citep{jauhiainen2019cuneiform}, the matching of fragments to known compositions by sign
sequences \citep{simonjetz2024reconstruction}, and palaeographic style \citep{yugay2024stylistic}.
Fragment matching finds a known text; it does not place a fragment whose parallels are lost, and
all of these studies are closed-set. We know of no published study that uses content features to
assign Hittite fragments to religious traditions, or that asks when a fragment fits none of them.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Corpus.}
We use the TLHdig Beta 0.3 release \citep{tlhdig03}, converted to one row per word following
\citet{yavasan2025clay}. Each word carries its normalised form, its transliteration split into
signs, its language, whether a sign is an Akkadogram, a Sumerogram or a determinative (TLHdig
tags these separately), and the lexical and morphological analyses that TLHdig produces
automatically with the HFR tool, which are not validated by hand \citep{tlhdig03}. The release is
downloaded by script and checked by its checksum, so the word table can be rebuilt from the public
data alone. As in \citet{gordin2025matters}, reference manuscripts must have at least 17 legible
words. The analyses do not cover the whole release: the Luwian rituals (\CTH{758}--763) have
none, and many other rituals lack them. The main model reads analysed words, so in its
reference the Luwian rituals have \Q{R1.A.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla.n} manuscripts and the
western rituals \Q{R1.A.arzawawesternrituals.n}; Section~\ref{sec:spellings} repeats the
evaluation on spellings alone with every manuscript.

\paragraph{Damage and restoration.}
TLHdig prints the editors' restorations alongside what survives, and marks each sign as preserved,
damaged, restored, omitted or lost. Counting restorations as text would let an editor's
reconstruction, often made from a duplicate, decide where a fragment belongs. We therefore count
only what is on the tablet. A sign counts if it is preserved or damaged, also when its reading is
uncertain; a word counts if at least half its signs survive, or if the same word form survives at
least half-preserved elsewhere in the same text. Restorations supported only by duplicates are
not used. Sign n-grams never span a missing sign, and a spelling counts only when every sign of
the word survives. We report the editors' full text, restorations included, as a comparison
(\term{version A}); the main results use the damage-aware text (\term{version C}).

\paragraph{Orphans.}
Orphans are all \CTH{470} manuscripts and all EBo manuscripts, wherever TLHdig files them,
\Q{orph.orphans_total} in all. \CTH{670} and 832, where most EBo fragments are filed, are pools of
orphans, not traditions. Of the \Q{corpus.orphans_total} orphans in the CTH ranges we load,
\Q{corpus.orphans_used} have at least five legible words and are placed (median
\Q{corpus.orphan_median} words). Orphans never enter the reference.

\paragraph{Reference traditions.}
Each tradition is defined by the study that argued for it (Table~\ref{tab:traditions}). The
Hurrian festival groups follow Table~3 of \citet{gordin2025matters}. Its single Luwian group is
split as HFR splits it, into the festivals of Ištanuwa and Lallupiya and those of Ḫuwaššanna, and
HFR's Palaic festivals are added \citep{hfr}. The ritual traditions are the Kizzuwatna rituals as
delimited by \citet{miller2004studies}; the western rituals, that is, the augur rituals
\citep{bawanypeck2005auguren} and those whose colophon names Arzawa or Ḫapalla as the
practitioner's home; the Luwian rituals of Puriyanni, Kuwattalla and Šilalluḫi
(\CTH{758}--763), which \citet{yakubovich2023luwili} place in the Lower Land rather than in
Kizzuwatna; and the Hattian cult layer of \citet{klinger1996hattische}, whose texts we take from
the citations of his study in the Mainz Konkordanz \citep{konkordanz}. The Ambazzi rituals
(\CTH{391}, 429, 463) show Luwian influence but no stated link to Arzawa
\citep{christiansen2006ambazzi}, so they stay outside the western group; Section~\ref{sec:recovery}
tests that decision.

Two sets leave the reference and are placed like orphans: the texts of \CTH{390}--469 that no
defining study claims, and \CTH{500}, ``fragments of festival and incantation rituals from
Kizzuwatna'', which is a running list of fragments rather than a composition. For \CTH{701},
the subgroups a--f of \citet{salvini1986chs} and the script dates come from the Konkordanz and
serve as partial ground truth.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}


Figure~\ref{fig:case} follows one orphan, \C{txtid}, through the procedure with the actual values
at each step.

\paragraph{Features.}
Every manuscript is represented twice \citep{salton1988weighting}. The word space holds tf-idf
weights of normalised word forms and stands for \term{what} is written; the sign space holds
tf-idf weights of sign n-grams ($n=1$--3) within words and stands for \term{how} it is written.
The main model concatenates both. Weights are sublinear, rows are L2-normalised, and features
found in fewer than two manuscripts are dropped. \C{txtid} has \C{words} words that count,
which yield \C{nfeat} non-zero features out of \C{dim}.

\paragraph{Assignment and abstention.}
Each tradition is represented by the normalised centroid of its manuscripts, and a fragment is
scored by its cosine similarity to every centroid. It is placed in the most similar tradition
when its lead over the runner-up, the \term{margin}, reaches a threshold $\tau$, and is left
unplaced otherwise. We choose $\tau$ on held-out data rather than by hand: manuscripts with at
least 40 words (at most 40 per tradition) are held out in turn and cut into ten random windows of
$k \in \{3,5,8,10,17,25,40\}$ words, and $\tau$ is the smallest margin at which these windows
reach \Q{E2.precision} precision. This gives $\tau=\Q{E2.margin}$ (90\% bootstrap interval
\Q{E2.margin.lo}--\Q{E2.margin.hi}). \C{txtid} is closest to the \C{topshort} (\C{s1}) and
next to the \C{secondshort} (\C{s2}); its margin of \C{margin} is well above $\tau$, and so it is
placed. The words that decide are the logograms of the AZU priest, the offering and the table, and
\C{nn.mode} of the five nearest single manuscripts belong to his rituals (\CTH{\C{nn.modecth}};
Figure~\ref{fig:case}, panel 6).

The orphan KBo 41.22 shows the other side. On the editor's full text (version A) it is placed
with the \Q{A.case.kbo4122.top}, by a lead of \Q{A.case.kbo4122.margin} on
\Q{A.case.kbo4122.words} words. Its decisive words, a formula with DINGIR.MEŠ and
\textit{karpin}, are the editor's restorations: on the tablet only \Q{case.kbo4122.words} words
remain, its similarity to the Luwian rituals falls to \Q{case.kbo4122.luwian}, and its lead of
\Q{case.kbo4122.margin} (\Q{case.kbo4122.top} over \Q{case.kbo4122.second}) is below
$\tau$: it is \Q{case.kbo4122.placed}. A placement can owe much to restorations, which is why the
main results count only what is on the tablet.

\paragraph{Decomposing a decision.}
Because the score is a dot product, the margin splits exactly into one term per feature,
$x_f(c_{\mathrm{top},f}-c_{2,f})$, where $x$ is the fragment and $c$ the two centroids. Summing
these terms by word category or layer shows what a placement rests on, and listing the largest
ones gives the words a philologist can check (Figure~\ref{fig:case}, panels 5--6). This matters
because similarity and decision differ: common words such as \textit{nu} make up much of a
fragment's similarity to every tradition but little of the difference between two.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Validation is leave-one-manuscript-out: each reference manuscript is removed from its own centroid
(in closed form) and reassigned. We report accuracy and per-tradition recall against two
baselines, always guessing the largest tradition (majority) and the same procedure with shuffled
labels (200 permutations, which also give a $p$-value). Idf weights are fitted on the whole
reference set.

\paragraph{Layers and word categories.}
To separate cultural content from scribal habit, the word space is split into layers, each
evaluated alone: logograms, Hittite vocabulary, foreign-language words (Hurrian, Luwian, Hattian,
Palaic), and the \Q{E7.variable_words} words attested in more than one spelling, once with their
spelling kept and once collapsed to the normalised form. If spelling carried tradition beyond
the word itself, the first would beat the second. We also use the word categories of
\citet[Table~4]{gordin2025matters}: agents, deities, function words, foreign words, realia and
verbs, with realia narrowed to physical things and the remaining nouns grouped by meaning
(places; animals, body parts and nature; abstract nouns; adjectives; measures).

No word was classed by hand. Fixed rules derive each category from TLHdig's markup, applied in
order: the word's determinative (DINGIR makes a deity, \textsuperscript{LÚ} an agent), its
language tag, and its automatic morphological analysis, using the analysis marked as selected
where there is one (a person-marked form is a verb, a particle or pronoun a function word);
unanalysed words fall back on the analyses selected for the same form elsewhere. Nouns and
adjectives are then classed by their German gloss, through a list of \Q{wc.glosses} glosses that
was drafted with a language model and has not been checked by hand; it is the one step not
derived from TLHdig. Each label carries the confidence of the rule that set it: \Q{wc.conf.high} of the
\Q{wc.tokens} legible words are classed with high confidence, \Q{wc.conf.medium} with medium and
\Q{wc.conf.low} with low. Since neither the automatic analyses nor our rules are validated in
TLHdig, our audit is the only manual check: a domain expert checked a stratified sample of \Q{wc.audit.n} words:
\Q{wc.audit.before.high}, \Q{wc.audit.before.medium} and \Q{wc.audit.before.low} were correct at
the three levels, \Q{wc.audit.before.corpus} overall when weighted by corpus shares. Fixing the
\Q{wc.audit.fixes} rules that the errors pointed to raises the overall figure to
\Q{wc.audit.after.corpus}, an upper bound since it is measured on the same sample. The audit
scored all nominal words as one class, so it does not cover the split by meaning.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Validation and fragment size}
\label{sec:validation}
Held out one at a time, \Q{E1.both.acc} of the \Q{E1.both.n} reference manuscripts return to
their tradition, against \Q{E1.both.majority} for the majority baseline and
\Q{E1.both.null_mean} for shuffled labels ($p=\Q{E1.both.p}$). Words and signs alone do almost as
well (\Q{E1.words.acc} and \Q{E1.signs.acc}). On the editor's full text (version A) the same
test gives \Q{A.E1.both.acc} on \Q{A.E1.both.n} manuscripts: restorations add manuscripts and
words but change little. Recall ranges from \Q{trad.minrecall} to
\Q{trad.maxrecall} (Table~\ref{tab:summary}) and is lowest for the two smallest traditions. The
festivals that HFR separates are kept apart by the texts themselves: the Ḫuwaššanna festivals
reach \Q{trad.huwassannafestivals.recall} and those of Ištanuwa and Lallupiya
\Q{trad.luwianfestivalsistanuwalallupiya.recall}.

Accuracy rises steeply with fragment size and then flattens. Windows of 3, 10 and 40 words cut
from held-out manuscripts are placed correctly in \Q{E2.both.k3}, \Q{E2.both.k10} and
\Q{E2.both.k40} of cases, against a chance level of \Q{chance}. Above the margin
$\tau=\Q{E2.margin}$, \Q{E2.share} of the windows are placed, at \Q{E2.precision} precision. In
version A, where every restored word counts, the same precision is reached at $\tau=\Q{A.E2.margin}$
and \Q{A.E2.share} of windows pass.

Inside \CTH{701}, the procedure recovers \Q{E3.both.acc} of the \Q{E3.both.n} manuscripts that
\citet{salvini1986chs} sorted into \Q{E3.both.classes} subgroups (majority \Q{E3.both.majority},
$p=\Q{E3.both.p}$). The same features do not predict script date (\Q{E3date.both.acc} against a
majority of \Q{E3date.both.majority}), so the subgroups are not clusters of date.

\subsection{Testing scholarly groupings}
\label{sec:recovery}
Leave-one-manuscript-out accuracy is flattered by duplicates: when one copy of a composition is
held out, its other copies stay in the reference. A stricter test removes a whole composition
(all manuscripts of one CTH number), rebuilds the centroids, and asks whether the composition
returns to the group that scholarship assigned it to. \Q{E10a.compositions_correct} of
\Q{E10a.compositions} compositions do, and \Q{E10a.texts_correct} of \Q{E10a.texts} manuscripts
(\Q{E10a.texts_share}) are placed correctly when their composition is unseen
(Table~\ref{tab:summary}). The Hattian cult layer, the Ḫuwaššanna festivals and the Kizzuwatna
rituals are recovered as groups. The Hurrian festival groups (\Q{E10a.hurrian} together), the
Palaic festivals and the two small ritual groups are not: their manuscripts resemble each other
because they share compositions, not because their compositions share a profile that sets them
apart. Held out whole, the \CTH{701} manuscripts go to the \Q{E10a.cth701.pred}
(\Q{E10a.cth701.texts} to their own group): in content, the rituals of the AZU priest are
Kizzuwatna rituals. We read the groups that are not recovered as claims that these features do
not support, not as claims that the evidence refutes.

\paragraph{A contested attribution.}
The Ambazzi rituals (\CTH{\Q{E10b.cths}}) have been read as western because of their Luwian
elements \citep{christiansen2006ambazzi}. We pool their \Q{E10b.texts} manuscripts and score them
against every tradition, comparing each tradition's lead over the best other with the same
statistic for random sets of the same size drawn from the \Q{E10b.pool} unclaimed ritual
manuscripts (\CTH{390}--469). The western tradition ranks \Q{E10b.claim.rank} of
\Q{trad.count} ($p=\Q{E10b.claim.p}$). The nearest tradition is the \Q{E10b.best}, for
\Q{E10b.nearest.n} of the \Q{E10b.texts} manuscripts, but no nearer than random unclaimed rituals
($p=\Q{E10b.best.p}$). Of the \Q{E10c.claim.signatures} words most distinctive of the western
rituals \citep{monroe2008fightin}, \Q{E10c.claim.attested} occurs in the Ambazzi texts
(\textit{\Q{E10c.claim.attested.words}}). On this evidence the Ambazzi rituals sit in the
generic ritual residue, and we keep them out of the western reference.

\subsection{Spellings only, with every manuscript}
\label{sec:spellings}
The gap in the annotation falls on the smallest traditions. We therefore repeat the
leave-one-out evaluation in the sign space alone, where every legible word counts, once with the
analysed manuscripts only and once with all manuscripts of 17 or more legible words
(Table~\ref{tab:spellings}). The reference grows from \Q{R1.A.n} to \Q{R1.B.n} manuscripts, and
overall accuracy falls from \Q{R1.A.acc} to \Q{R1.B.acc}, because most traditions gain
manuscripts that are harder to place. The two small ritual traditions gain most: the Luwian
rituals grow from \Q{R1.A.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla.n} to
\Q{R1.B.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla.n} manuscripts and their accuracy from
\Q{R1.A.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla.acc} to \Q{R1.B.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla.acc};
the western rituals from \Q{R1.A.arzawawesternrituals.n} to \Q{R1.B.arzawawesternrituals.n}
(\Q{R1.A.arzawawesternrituals.acc} to \Q{R1.B.arzawawesternrituals.acc}). Of the
\Q{R1.B.orph.mainplaced} orphans the main model places, \Q{R1.B.orph.placedinboth} are also placed
with the full reference and \Q{R1.B.orph.sametradition} of these keep their tradition;
\Q{R1.B.orph.topuriyannikuwattalla} move to the Luwian rituals and \Q{R1.B.orph.toarzawa} to the
western rituals. Placements in the two small ritual traditions should be read with this in
mind.

\subsection{What a placement rests on}
\label{sec:decision}
\paragraph{What is written, or how.}
Each layer alone identifies a tradition (Table~\ref{tab:layers}). Logograms reach
\Q{E7.logograms.acc}, Hittite vocabulary \Q{E7.lexicon.acc}, and foreign-language words
\Q{E7.foreign.acc} in the \Q{E7.foreign.n} manuscripts that have them. The variable words reach
\Q{E7.spelling.acc} with their spelling kept and \Q{E7.collapsed.acc} with it collapsed: the
choice of spelling adds nothing beyond the word. The seven orthographic rates are a weak but real
signal (\Q{E7.orthographic_indices.acc} against \Q{E7.orthographic_indices.null_mean} for

almost unchanged (\Q{E8.without_function.words}, against \Q{E8.all.words} with all words), and on their own they
reach \Q{E8.only_function.words}, below agents (\Q{E8.only_agent.words}) and foreign words
(\Q{E8.only_foreign.words}). Removing the foreign words costs most

whom and what they name.

\paragraph{Similarity is not decision.}
Splitting each decision over its features (Section~\ref{sec:method}) answers an obvious worry:
the particle \textit{nu} and other frequent words weigh heavily in a fragment's similarity to its
tradition. Over the \Q{E11a.texts} correctly placed reference manuscripts, features found in at
least a quarter of the reference make up \Q{E11b.common.similarity} of the similarity but only
\Q{E11b.common.ref} of the decision, because every centroid shares them and they largely cancel.
Deleting the 100 most frequent words, \Q{E11c.k100.removed} of all word tokens, leaves accuracy at
\Q{E11c.k100.acc}. The decision rests on agents and deities (\Q{E11a.decision.agentsanddeities}),
realia (\Q{E11a.decision.realia}), foreign words (\Q{E11a.decision.foreignwords}) and verbs
(\Q{E11a.decision.verbs}); function words carry \Q{E11a.decision.functionwords}. Each tradition
has its own decisive category (Table~\ref{tab:summary}): realia for the Kizzuwatna rituals,
agents and deities for the festivals and the Hattian cult layer.

For short fragments the worry is justified. The share of the decision carried by common features
falls from \Q{E11b.len510.common} for orphans of 5--10 words to \Q{E11b.len50.common} above 50
words, and \Q{E11b.assigned_orphans_mostly_common} of the \Q{E11b.assigned_orphans} placed
orphans rest mostly on them. In 10-word windows from reference manuscripts, such placements are
right \Q{E11d.w10.common.prec} of the time, against \Q{E11d.w10.rest.prec} for the rest. Without
function words, \Q{E11e.nofunc.sametradition} of the placed orphans keep their tradition,
\Q{E11e.nofunc.othertradition} move and \Q{E11e.nofunc.nowunassigned} fall below the margin.
Common words thus rarely send a fragment to the wrong tradition, but they often supply the margin
that lets it be placed at all. We therefore release the decomposition with every placement and
treat one that rests mostly on common features as weak.

\subsection{Orphans and the open set}
\label{sec:orphans}
With the calibrated margin, \Q{E5.assigned} of the \Q{corpus.orphans_used} orphans with five or
more legible words are placed (\Q{E5.assigned.lo}--\Q{E5.assigned.hi} across the margin's 90\%
interval), \Q{E5.top.share} of them with the \Q{E5.top}. The test sets show why this needs care.
The named-practitioner rituals of \CTH{390}--469, which no study assigns to Kizzuwatna, are
placed mostly with the Kizzuwatna rituals (\Q{E5b.res.kizz} of \Q{E5b.res.assigned}), the only
large ritual class: genre before origin. \CTH{500} behaves differently: \Q{E5b.c500.hurrian} of
its \Q{E5b.c500.assigned} placed fragments go to the Hurrian festival groups, in line with HFR's
treatment of \CTH{500} as festival material \citep{hfr}.

A closed reference always names a tradition \citep{koppel2011wild}. When windows of \Q{E9.k}
words from \Q{E9.genres.count} other genres are scored, the margin rule still places
\Q{E9.genres.placed.lo}--\Q{E9.genres.placed.hi} of them, and \Q{E9.fest.placed} of windows from
festivals of other traditions. Adding the outside genres as classes of their own (a genre gate)
recognises \Q{E9.genres.gate.lo}--\Q{E9.genres.gate.hi} of their windows and \Q{E9.fest.gate} of
the other festivals, at the cost of sending \Q{E9.ref.gate} of reference windows away. Applied to
the orphans, the gate finds \Q{E9.orph.outside} that are closer to an outside genre than to any
tradition by more than the margin, \Q{E9.orph.assigned_outside} of them among the placed. We
release them as candidates for re-filing.

The damage-aware text places far fewer orphans than the editor's full text (\Q{A.E5.assigned} of
\Q{A.corpus.orphans_used} in version A), because many orphans lose most of their words with the
restorations. The placements that remain are stable: of the \Q{AC.placedA.scoredC} orphans placed in
version A that are still scored in C, \Q{AC.placedA.samenearest} (\Q{AC.placedA.samenearest.share})
keep the same nearest tradition. The loss is in confidence, not in direction.

\subsection{Duplicates, not joins: a negative result}
\label{sec:joins}
A fragment's nearest manuscripts are leads for the philologist, and the question is what kind of
lead they are. Against the Konkordanz, the ranking finds duplicates: of \Q{KJ.duplicate.pairs}
recorded duplicate pairs, the partner ranks first in \Q{KJ.duplicate.top1} and among the 20
nearest of \Q{KJ.pool} manuscripts in \Q{KJ.duplicate.top20} (median rank
\Q{KJ.duplicate.median}). On the editor's full text the figures look better (median rank
\Q{A.KJ.duplicate.median}, \Q{A.KJ.duplicate.top20} of \Q{A.KJ.duplicate.pairs} in the top 20),
but partly by circularity: editors restore duplicates from each other. Of the
\Q{KJ.join.pairs} suspected joins, only \Q{KJ.join.top20} reaches the top 20 (median rank
\Q{KJ.join.median}). Two pieces of one tablet share their context but, as
a rule, not their text, so we tried to learn a ranking for joins (run so far on version A).

TLHdig marks on every line of a joined text the piece it is preserved on. Splitting its joined
texts back into their pieces gives \Q{J1.query_pieces} query pieces from \Q{J1.joined_texts}
joins, each to be found among \Q{J1.units} units. A logistic regression re-ranks the nearest
candidates of each piece over twelve pair features: similarity, the similarity of the two
units' profiles over traditions and over compositions, word and phrase overlap, the share of
shared words spelled alike, the distance of their orthographic rates, the overlap of their
preserved sides and columns, the same publication volume, the same excavation series, and size.
It is tested on compositions it was not fitted on (five folds grouped by CTH number). The gain is
small (Table~\ref{tab:joins}): the partner reaches the top 20 for \Q{J1.model.r20} of pieces,
against \Q{J1.sim.r20} for similarity alone, although the median rank falls from
\Q{J1.sim.median} to \Q{J1.model.median}. On the Konkordanz's suspected joins the model places
\Q{J1.konk.join.model.hits20} of \Q{J1.konk.join.queries} in the top 20, against
\Q{J1.konk.join.sim.hits20}.

The weights show why. Similarity (\Q{J1.w.cosine}) and the shared tradition and composition
profiles (\Q{J1.w.traditioncos}, \Q{J1.w.compositioncos}) favour a join, as do identical spellings
(\Q{J1.w.samespelling}) and preserved columns (\Q{J1.w.columnoverlap}); word overlap counts
against it (\Q{J1.w.wordoverlap}), a weak sign of the expected complementarity. The scribal rates
have no measurable effect (\Q{J1.w.scribaldistance}, 95\% interval \Q{J1.w.scribaldistance.ci}).
The joins already made are those scholars could see, mostly pieces that share text, so they
teach a model what duplicates look like. Find spot and date, the evidence one would want, cannot
be fitted honestly, because for known joins the Konkordanz often derives them from the join
itself. Text alone, we conclude, finds parallels and duplicates but does not tell a join from
them; that needs physical evidence.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Genre before origin.}
A fragment is placed by what it shares with the reference, and the largest shared layer is
genre. A ritual from outside every reference tradition will look most like the largest ritual
class, as the residue of \CTH{390}--469 shows, and the open-set test shows the same risk across
genres. A placement is therefore ``nearest of these'', and is only as good as the alternatives in
the reference. Each tradition added narrows the gap: with the Luwian rituals in the reference,
\Q{E5b.res.luwianritualspuriyannikuwattalla} residue texts go to them rather than to the
Kizzuwatna rituals, and the spellings-only run shows that the small traditions gain most when
their unanalysed manuscripts are counted.

\paragraph{Archetypes and witnesses.}
The results suggest a division of labour between two levels of a text, which we call
Quantitative Scribal Tradition Mapping (QTM). Borrowing the terms of textual criticism
\citep{maas1958textual,trovato2014lachmann}, a tradition is an \term{archetype}: not a
reconstructed text, but the vocabulary, formulae and foreign-language recitation that its
manuscripts share. Each manuscript, and each orphan, is a \term{witness} that attests part of
it. The archetype is identified by content, above all by whom and what a text names. Spelling
marks the witness: kept or collapsed, the variant spellings change nothing at the level of the
tradition, in line with the view that Hittite writing habits belong to scribal circles and their
schooling \citep{gordin2015scribal}. An orphan is a low-coverage witness, and how much of each
archetype survives only in orphans is a historical question that unseen-species estimates, as
used for lost medieval literature \citep{kestemont2022forgotten}, could address.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Content-based assignment places Hittite ritual and festival fragments in literature-defined
traditions well above chance from about ten legible words, recovers most compositions held out
whole, and gives a quantitative answer to a contested attribution. The exact decomposition makes
each placement checkable: it shows that a decision rests on agents, deities, realia and foreign
words rather than on spelling or particles, and flags the short fragments where it does not. The
same features find duplicates but not joins (Section~\ref{sec:joins}). Code, outputs and the
placements with their decompositions are released (anonymised for review); every number in this
paper is generated from them.

\section*{Limitations}
The study depends on one digital edition. TLHdig 0.3 has no word analyses for the Luwian
corpus and for many rituals, its morphological analyses are automatic and unvalidated, and its
normalised forms are uneven across editors. Words with less than half their signs on the tablet
are dropped unless the same text supports them, so short fragments over-represent the formulaic
lines that survive, and the half-preserved threshold is a choice we have not varied. Several traditions are small
(the smallest has \Q{trad.smallest.n} test manuscripts), their recall is uncertain, and held out
by composition several are not recovered at all. The traditions themselves are taken from the
literature: the method tests them, but cannot find a tradition that no study has proposed. The
idf weights are fitted on the whole reference set. Festival manuscripts also differ by function
(outline tablets, day tablets, inventories close to festival text \citep{cammarosano2021inventories}),
which word profiles may track alongside tradition. The word categories enter only the analysis of
what a decision rests on, not the placements; their rules were checked on a sample, but the
meaning classes of nouns rest on a gloss list drafted with a language model that is still to be
checked by hand. Finally, every placement is a hypothesis for verification on the tablet; a
fragment placed near a known manuscript is a candidate, not a join.
